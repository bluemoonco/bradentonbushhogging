Straight answers

Questions we get every week

How much does bush hogging cost per acre in Bradenton?

It depends on four things: how tall the growth is, how much sapling and palmetto is mixed into it, how wet the ground is, and how hard it is to get a tractor onto the parcel. Small residential lots get a flat job price. Open acreage is priced per acre, and the per-acre rate drops as the acreage goes up. Either way you get a firm number before we start — not an hourly meter.

How fast can you get out here?

Quote requests get answered the same business day. Most Manatee County jobs land on the schedule inside the same week. If you’re working against a code enforcement deadline or a closing date, say so in the form — those move to the front.

How tall and how thick can you actually cut?

Grass and brush up to head height, and saplings up to roughly two to three inches at the base. Beyond that — mature trees, stumps, palmetto root mat that has to come out rather than be cut down — you need a different machine, and we’ll tell you that up front instead of beating up equipment on your property and handing you a bill.

Do I have to be there?

No. A lot of our customers are out-of-state owners, investors and realtors who’ve never set foot on the parcel. We work off the parcel ID and boundaries, cut it, and send photos when it’s finished.

I got a Manatee County overgrown-lot notice. Can you help?

Yes — that’s one of the most common calls we take in Bradenton and Palmetto. Attach or mention the compliance date in your request and we’ll schedule to beat it.

What do you do with the cuttings?

A rotary cutter mulches material back onto the ground, so standard bush hogging leaves the cuttings in place. If you need debris hauled off, or the lot cut low enough for a survey crew, tell us and we’ll price that in.
